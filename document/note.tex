\section{Method}

\subsection{isoTNS representation}

We consider the toric code on a square lattice with open boundary conditions,
\[
H=-\sum_s A_s-\sum_p B_p, \qquad
A_s=\prod_{i\in s}\sigma_i^z, \qquad
B_p=\prod_{i\in p}\sigma_i^x.
\]

\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.4\textwidth]{image/lattice_diagram.pdf}
  \caption{Square-lattice geometry of the planar code with open boundaries.}
  \label{fig:planar-lattice}
\end{figure}

Each bond is represented by a doubled Hilbert space, \(\lvert i\rangle\mapsto
\lvert ii\rangle\). The local rank-eight tensor is
\[
T_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}^{ijkl}
=
\left\{
\begin{aligned}
  \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,
  \delta_{i\alpha}\delta_{j\beta}\delta_{k\gamma}\delta_{l\delta},
  &\quad \text{if } \alpha+\beta+\gamma+\delta=0 \pmod{2}, \\
  0, &\quad \text{otherwise}.
\end{aligned}
\right.
\]
The physical and virtual indices are ordered as east, north, west, and south.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.4\textwidth]{image/lattice_diagram_double_Hilbert_space.pdf}
  \caption{Planar-code lattice in the doubled-Hilbert-space representation. Each virtual bond is doubled as \(\lvert i\rangle\mapsto\lvert ii\rangle\), and \(T_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}^{ijkl}\) labels the local rank-eight tensor.}
  \label{fig:doubled-planar-lattice}
\end{figure}

Independent bit-flip errors occur on the internal virtual bonds with
probability \(p\). A distance-\(d\) patch contains \(2d(d-1)\) such bonds.

\subsubsection{Boundary conditions and gate schedule}

The planar patch has two rough and two smooth boundaries and encodes one
logical qubit. Physical qubits are initialized in \(\lvert 0\rangle\). The
virtual boundary convention is \(\lvert +\rangle\) on the west and east
boundaries and \(\lvert 0\rangle\) on the south and north boundaries. At the
east and north boundaries, the terminal carrier is absorbed into the local
gate.

There are four local-gate types: a six-qubit bulk gate, five-qubit east and
north boundary gates, and a four-qubit corner gate. Each gate satisfies the
isometry condition. Gates are applied in diagonal layers, progressing from
south to north and from west to east.

\subsection{Sampling and virtual-bond diagnostics}

The circuit admits a classical message-passing description. The Yao.jl
implementation is a small-system reference: it applies each local gate to a
state vector, performs a Born measurement, records the result, and resets the
measured physical qubits. The production estimator instead works directly
with batches of virtual-bond error frames. For each shot, it samples
independent horizontal and vertical bond-error bits with probability \(p\),
then evaluates the detector record and west-boundary logical parity by XOR
operations. The full detector batch is decoded once with PyMatching, and a
logical failure is counted when the predicted logical parity differs from the
sampled one. No unitary gates or individual physical measurement outcomes are
simulated.



\subsubsection{Virtual-bond noise checks}

For an \(L\times L\) lattice, with \(L=d\), the following quantities diagnose
the virtual-bond noise and its percolation behaviour:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textit{Injected-error and mismatch densities:}
  \begin{align*}
  \rho_{\mathrm{injected}}
  &=\frac{\text{number of erroneous virtual bonds}}{2L(L-1)+4L}\approx p, \\
  \rho_{\mathrm{mismatch}}
  &=\frac{\text{number of mismatched internal doubled bonds}}{2L(L-1)}\approx p.
  \end{align*}

  \item \textit{Plaquette frustration:} the probability that a plaquette has
  odd error parity is
  \[
  P_{\mathrm{odd}}
  =\binom{4}{1}p(1-p)^3+\binom{4}{3}p^3(1-p)
  =\frac{1}{2}\left[1-(1-2p)^4\right].
  \]

  \item \textit{Largest occupied cluster:} the normalized cluster size is
  \(S_{\max}/L^2\), where \(S_{\max}\) is the number of sites in the largest
  connected error cluster.

  \item \textit{Horizontal spanning probability:}
  \[
  P_{\mathrm{span}}^{\mathrm{horizontal}}
  =P(\text{an error cluster connects the west and east boundaries}).
  \]
\end{enumerate}


\subsection{Syndrome extraction}

A detector is a binary indicator of whether an error violates an expected
parity constraint. Let \(\mathbf e\) denote the binary virtual-bond error
vector. The detector record is
\[
\mathbf s=\mathsf H\mathbf e \pmod{2},
\]
where \(\mathsf H\) is the parity-check matrix. Equivalently,
\[
s_i=
\left\{
\begin{aligned}
  0, &\quad \text{if an even number of adjacent bonds have errors}, \\
  1, &\quad \text{if an odd number of adjacent bonds have errors}.
\end{aligned}
\right.
\]

For bit-flip noise, we use two detector families:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item an elementary plaquette detector,
  \[
  S_{r,c}=h_{r,c}\oplus h_{r+1,c}\oplus v_{r,c}\oplus v_{r,c+1};
  \]
  \item north and south boundary detectors,
  \[
  n_c=h_{1,c}, \qquad s_c=h_{d,c}.
  \]
\end{enumerate}
The total number of detectors is
\((d-1)^2+2(d-1)=d^2-1\).

\subsection{Decoding and logical-failure estimation}

PyMatching returns both a minimum-weight correction and the logical parity
associated with that correction. Let \(E\) be the physical error, \(C\) the
decoder correction, and \(R=E\oplus C\) the residual error. A logical failure
occurs when
\[
l(R)=l(E)\oplus l(C)=1.
\]

The finite-size scaling ansatz for the logical-failure probability is
\[
P_{\mathrm{fail}}(d,p)\simeq F\!\left((p-p_c)d^{1/\nu}\right).
\]
For each \((d,p)\) point and each batch, we:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item sample all \(2d(d-1)\) virtual-bond errors for multiple shots;
  \item compute the \(d^2-1\) detector values algebraically;
  \item compute the hidden west-boundary logical parity;
  \item decode the batch with PyMatching; and
  \item count disagreements between the actual and predicted logical parities.
\end{enumerate}
For \(N\) shots with \(F\) logical failures,
\[
P_{\mathrm{fail}}=\frac{F}{N},
\qquad
\mathrm{SE}(P_{\mathrm{fail}})
=\sqrt{\frac{P_{\mathrm{fail}}(1-P_{\mathrm{fail}})}{N}}.
\]


\subsection{Bootstrap uncertainty}

To quantify the uncertainty of a crossing estimate, we partition the shots at
each \((d,p)\) point into equal batches. Each bootstrap replicate resamples
these batch failure rates with replacement, recomputes the monotone
logical-failure curves for adjacent code distances, and extracts their unique
crossing. The confidence interval is obtained from the corresponding empirical
quantiles of the valid bootstrap crossings.

\section{error model}

apply X-only or Z-only error after each elementary gate (H, CNOT) with rate $p$

\section{sampling}

Monte Carlo sampling chooses which faults occur; the Pauli-frame simulator determines their effect on the circuit.

For each code distance \(d\) and physical error probability \(p\):

1. Sample faults. After every gate, independently apply an X fault with probability \(p\) to each participating qubit. Each CNOT therefore has two possible fault locations.

2. Propagate errors. Track the X/Z error pattern through the remaining circuit using exact Clifford rules,
   \[
   E\longrightarrow UEU^\dagger.
   \]This accounts for errors spreading and cancelling, without storing the full wavefunction.

3. Decode and judge success. Calculate the final, ideal stabilizer outcomes and give them to the matching decoder. Compare its proposed correction with the actual propagated error. For preparation of \(|0_L\rangle\), a remaining logical X counts as failure.

4. Repeat and estimate. Run \(N\) independent trials and count \(F\) failures:
   \[
   \boxed{\widehat P_L(d,p)=\frac{F}{N}.}
   \]
Error propagation is exact for the Clifford circuit and Pauli-noise model; the estimated failure probability has finite-sampling uncertainty.

\section{3D toric code}

\subsection{Noise and logical observables}

Each active H/CNOT output independently acquires an X or Z fault with
probability $p$ in separate single-channel experiments. Initialization,
idles, measurements, and corrections are ideal unless stated otherwise.
Compared protocols reuse the same fault records. Final recovery uses
MWPM for Z and BP+OSD for X; complete recoveries must reproduce the syndrome.

Logical failure is the union of the scored residual logical-Pauli bits.
For $|0_L\rangle$ or $|00_L\rangle$, only logical X is preparation failure;
logical Z is a Pauli-frame diagnostic. For $|++\rangle$, logical Z is
state failure. All threshold estimates below depend on the circuit,
decoder, and ideal-feedback assumptions.

\subsection{Cartesian sweep: geometry and Z-fault bound}

Use a cubic slab with rough open $x$ ends and periodic $y,z$ directions.
Retained vertices have $x=1,\ldots,L_x$; longitudinal edges have
$x=0,\ldots,L_x$. For $L_x\geq1$, $L_y,L_z\geq2$, and $M=L_yL_z$,
\[
 H=-\sum_v A_v-\sum_f B_f,\qquad
 A_v=\prod_{e\ni v}X_e,\quad B_f=\prod_{e\in\partial f}Z_e,
\]
\[
 n=(3L_x+1)M,\quad N_A=L_xM,\quad N_B=(3L_x+2)M,\quad
 \operatorname{rank}B=(2L_x+1)M-1,\quad k=1.
\]
Rough-end plaquettes are triangular, e.g.,
\[
 B^{xy}_{0,y,z}=Z^x_{0,y,z}Z^x_{0,y+1,z}Z^y_{1,y,z},\qquad
 B^{xy}_{L_x,y,z}=Z^x_{L_x,y,z}Z^x_{L_x,y+1,z}Z^y_{L_x,y,z}.
\]
Interior plaquettes have four edges; period-two parallel edges remain
distinct. The longitudinal Z string and transverse X membrane give
\[
 d_Z=L_x+1,\qquad d_X=M,\qquad
 |0_L\rangle=2^{N_A/2}\prod_v\frac{I+A_v}{2}|0\rangle^{\otimes n}.
\]

Visit vertices in increasing $(x,y,z)$ order. Apply H to the fresh outgoing
$x$-edge, then CNOTs from it to the incoming $x,+y,-y,+z,-z$ edges, in
that order. Final-only recovery acts once; layer feedback decodes each
completed prefix slab, including its moving triangular frontier, and
corrects only available qubits.

\paragraph{Indistinguishable fault pair.}
For each $(y,z)$, choose Z-fault bits $a$ on the left boundary edge after
its first CNOT and $b$ on the outgoing edge after the final CNOT of the
first-plane gadget. At checkpoint $j$, toggling both adds
\[
 D_j=\prod_{x=0}^{j}Z^x_{x,y,z},
\]
which has zero prefix syndrome and becomes logical Z at $j=L_x$.
Thus the syndrome history cannot distinguish the pair assignments with
the same $a\oplus b$. Even if all other faults and all $M$ pair XORs are
revealed, an odd XOR leaves unbiased logical parity. For an even XOR,
the parity bias is $(1-2p)/q$, where $q=(1-p)^2+p^2$. Hence, for
$0\leq p<1/2$ and any syndrome-only decoder,
\[
 P_{Z,\mathrm{diagnostic}}\geq
 \frac{1-q^M[(1-2p)/q]^M}{2}
 =\frac{1-(1-2p)^M}{2}.
\]
This bounds the Cartesian logical-Z diagnostic, not $|0_L\rangle$
infidelity or the diagonal circuits below.

With 10000 paired records per point at $L=3,5,7,9$ and
$p\in\{1,3,10,30,100,300\}\times10^{-6}$, the linearly interpolated
one-percent feedback diagnostic occurs at
\[
 \bigl(p_{1\%}(3),p_{1\%}(5),p_{1\%}(7),p_{1\%}(9)\bigr)
 =(1.39\times10^{-4},5.03\times10^{-5},2.92\times10^{-5},1.51\times10^{-5}).
\]
The fit $p_{1\%}(L)\propto L^{-1.97}$ has bootstrap exponent interval
$[1.77,2.17]$, consistent with the bound's vanishing diagnostic threshold.

\subsection{Diagonal H+5: direct vertex projectors}

Set $t=x+y+z$, $u=x$, $v=y$, with open $t=0,\ldots,T$ and periodic
$(u,v)\in\mathbb Z_{L_u}\times\mathbb Z_{L_v}$. Edge $E[d,t,r]$ joins
$V(t-1,r-s_d)$ to $V(t,r)$, where
$s_1=(1,0)$, $s_2=(0,1)$, $s_3=(0,0)$. The slab has
\[
 n=3(T+1)L_uL_v,\qquad k=2,\qquad
 d_Z=2\min(L_u,L_v),\quad d_X=(T+1)\min(L_u,L_v).
\]
Period-two edges remain distinct. For $V(t-1,r)$, write
$e_d=E[d,t-1,r]$ and $n_d=E[d,t,r+s_d]$.
The direct star-projector circuit is
\[
 H(n_1),\quad
 \mathrm{CX}(n_1,n_2),\quad\mathrm{CX}(n_1,n_3),\quad
 \mathrm{CX}(n_1,e_1),\quad\mathrm{CX}(n_1,e_2),\quad
 \mathrm{CX}(n_1,e_3).
\]
It has six gates and eleven noisy outputs per cell, implementing a
paper-motivated construction rather than a circuit reproduced from it.

Product zero fixes both logical-Z cycles. Ideally project and correct the
lower white X triads, run the bulk circuit, then project the upper X triads.
Their clean outcomes $b$ are uniform with even parity. If Z faults flip
them by $u$, the physical controller sees only $a=b\oplus u$. For its
deterministic correction $C$, the relative frame is
\[
 z'=z\oplus C(a)\oplus C(b).
\]
Use edge-ID-ordered breadth-first paths to $A_{T-1,0,0}$ after removing
both logical-X supports; this guarantees zero logical overlap. The clean
branch $b$ and fault record remain hidden from the decoders.

\paragraph{Feedback protocols.}
Reuse each fault record and boundary branch in four arms. Feedback occurs
after $t=1,\ldots,T-1$:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \texttt{none}: boundary completion and final recovery only.
\item \texttt{one\_step}: X uses MWPM on the periodic six-edge $Q_t$
checks and applies the first and last path edges (once for a length-one
path). Z matches the retiring/frontier star strip, adding one future dummy
only for odd parity, and corrects retiring endpoints only. Frontier
defects may remain.
\item \texttt{window\_complete}: recover on $E_{t-1}\cup E_t$ using
BP+OSD for completed plaquettes and open-boundary MWPM for the star strip.
All completed checks clear.
\item \texttt{full\_prefix}: recover from all completed checks on
$E_0,\ldots,E_t$; future qubits are unavailable.
\end{itemize}
The arms change measurements, decoder, residual constraints, and correction
support together. Their differences do not isolate correction range.
Final BP+OSD uses 50 minimum-sum iterations and \texttt{OSD\_CS} order two.
MWPM minimizes physical length with deterministic ties. Both omit
circuit-induced correlations.

\subsection{Historical H+6: plaquette transfer}

The seven-gate H+6 circuit transfers old controls to fresh target triads
on the diagonal slab above, with thirteen noisy outputs per cell.
A single Z output fault spreads to at most three qubits in two adjacent
slices. Its seed preparation and upper boundary instrument differ from
H+5, so their thresholds are not a controlled circuit-only comparison.

For X noise, prepare $|00\rangle$ using ideal seed X-star measurements,
with no noisy seed gates. One-step feedback matches defects of the old
slice's Z compatibility checks and feeds endpoint repairs forward to fresh
targets. Matching is global; this is not a constant-depth spatially local
channel. Final BP+OSD scores the union of logical-X membranes.

For Z noise, prepare $|++\rangle$ using noisy seed H gates followed by
ideal Z-compatibility measurements and X corrections. These preserve the
seed Z faults. Final MWPM scores the union of logical-Z windings. The
older Z scan's intermediate Z-check/X-correction feedback is inactive
under pure Z noise.

\paragraph{Separate sector estimates.}
For $T=L_u=L_v=L$, the Z scan uses $L=9,13,17,21,25$ and 10000 shots per point
(330000 total). A quadratic logistic fit with a fixed $1/L$ correction
gives $p_{c,Z}\simeq0.0052$. The $0.49\%$--$0.54\%$ range across fit
variants measures model sensitivity, not a confidence interval.

The X scan uses $L=5,7,9,11,13$, $p=0.0100$--$0.0250$, and 1000 paired
records per point (35000 total). Final-only curves have no adjacent-size
crossings in this window. The all-size fits fail the goodness guard;
the accepted cubic sensitivity fit for one-step feedback with $L\geq7$ gives
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 p_{c,X}=0.01482,\qquad \nu=1.056,\qquad
 p_{\mathrm{deviance}}=0.01996,\\
 p_{c,X}\in[0.01428,0.01543]\quad\text{(conditional 95\% interval)}.
 \end{gathered}
\]
Of 1000 bootstrap refits, 999 pass. These separate input-state and channel studies
do not establish a joint X/Z threshold or reproduce a threshold from the
paper.

\paragraph{Three feedback strengths.}
A separate paired scan compares no feedback, slice-local feedback, and
full-prefix recovery. X slice-local feedback uses the one-step rule above;
full-prefix X uses complete BP+OSD. Active Z feedback first corrects seed
X stars, then uses either a three-check-sheet band with MWPM on the newest
two data slices, or full-prefix MWPM. A single-slice Z window misses
old-control faults. Both active Z policies clear all completed stars.
Their paths and tie choices can differ. No feedback omits the seed-time
correction, so this comparison also changes more than recovery support.
